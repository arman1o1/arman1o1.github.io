Terrell–Penrose Rotation: What a 0.99c Flyby Actually Looks Like

November 26, 2026 • ☕ 10 min read

For over fifty years after Einstein published Special Relativity, textbooks drew a cube flying past at $0.9c$ as a squashed flat pancake. Then in 1959, James Terrell and Roger Penrose independently realized that measuring a Lorentz-contracted rod is completely different from photographing it. A camera records photons that arrive at the lens at the same instant $t_{\text{obs}}$ – which means photons from the farther back corners had to be emitted earlier, when the cube was further back along its track!

1. Solving the Exact Retarded Light-Cone Equation

Take a rest-frame vertex $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ on a cube moving along $+x$ at speed $\beta c$. At observer time $t_{\text{obs}}$, Lorentz contraction places it at $x_{\text{now}} = x_0 / \gamma + \beta c\, t_{\text{obs}}$. A photon arriving at the camera at the origin at $t_{\text{obs}}$ was emitted $\tau = t_{\text{obs}} - t_{\text{emit}} \gt 0$ seconds earlier, satisfying the light-cone condition:

Retarded Light-Cone Delay & Apparent Terrell Rotation Angle
$$c^2 \tau^2 = (x_{\text{now}} - \beta c \tau)^2 + y_0^2 + z_0^2 \implies (1 - \beta^2)(c\tau)^2 + 2\beta x_{\text{now}}(c\tau) - (x_{\text{now}}^2 + y_0^2 + z_0^2) = 0$$ $$\sin\theta_{\text{Terrell}} = \beta \qquad \big(\text{at } \beta = 0.90,\;\; \theta_{\text{Terrell}} = 64.2^\circ\big)$$

Because the back face of the cube is farther from the camera by side length $L$, its light takes $\Delta\tau = L/c$ longer to reach the lens – during which the cube moved forward by $\beta L$. That exact $\beta L$ offset matches the projected width of a cube rotated by $\theta = \arcsin\beta$, while the front face contracts by $1/\gamma = \cos\theta$. Together, the cube looks rigidly rotated so you literally see around its trailing corner!

💻 GLSL: Exact Retarded-Time Vertex ShaderGLSL / Retarded-Time Vertex Shader 
// Exact Retarded-Time Solver for a Vertex Moving at Velocity v = (beta, 0, 0) (units: c = 1)
// Solve: (t_obs - t_emit) = || (x0/gamma + beta*t_emit, y0, z0) - camPos ||
// Quadratic in tau = t_obs - t_emit >= 0:
// (1 - beta^2) * tau^2 + 2 * beta * dx_now * tau - (dx_now^2 + dy^2 + dz^2) = 0
float a = max(1e-5, 1.0 - beta * beta);
float b = 2.0 * beta * dx_now;
float c = -(dx_now*dx_now + dy*dy + dz*dz);
float tau = (-b + sqrt(max(0.0, b*b - 4.0*a*c))) / (2.0 * a);
vec3 apparentPos = vec3(x_now - beta * tau, y0, z0); // light-travel delay rotates the cube!
