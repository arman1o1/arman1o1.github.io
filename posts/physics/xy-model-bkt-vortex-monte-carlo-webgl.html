Topological Phase Transitions in Your Browser: The 2D XY Model & BKT Vortex Unbinding via Checkerboard Monte Carlo

November 19, 2026 • ☕ 12 min read

Every physics simulation we have built so far – from Gross–Pitaevskii superfluids to Cahn–Hilliard spinodal decomposition – evolved a deterministic PDE forward in time. Here we switch to a completely different computational paradigm on the GPU: stochastic Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) for the 2D classical XY spin model (Nguyen & Boninsegni, 2021, arXiv:2105.14112; arXiv:2407.17507).

By the Mermin–Wagner theorem, a 2D system with continuous $U(1)$ rotational symmetry cannot undergo conventional spontaneous symmetry breaking at any non-zero temperature $T \gt 0$ because long-wavelength spin waves destroy true long-range magnetization ($\langle \vec{s} \rangle = 0$). Yet in the early 1970s, Berezinskii (1971), Kosterlitz, and Thouless (1973) discovered an infinite-order topological phase transition at $T_{\text{BKT}} \approx 0.893\,J/k_B$ driven entirely by the unbinding of quantized $\pm 2\pi$ spin vortex–antivortex pairs!

0. Energy–Entropy Balance of a Single Topological Vortex & Checkerboard Detailed Balance

Assign each site $(i, j)$ on a 2D square lattice a unit planar spin $\vec{s}_i = (\cos\theta_i, \sin\theta_i)$ interacting with its four nearest neighbors via $H = -J \sum_{\langle i, j \rangle} \cos(\theta_i - \theta_j)$. A single isolated $+2\pi$ vortex on a lattice of size $L \times L$ costs elastic energy $E_v \approx \pi J \ln(L/a)$ while gaining configurational entropy $S_v = k_B \ln(L^2 / a^2) = 2 k_B \ln(L/a)$:

BKT Free Energy Unbinding Criterion & Plaquette Topological Winding Number
$$F_{\text{vortex}} = E_v - T S_v = (\pi J - 2 k_B T)\ln\!\left(\frac{L}{a}\right) \;\Longrightarrow\; T_{\text{BKT}}^{\text{mean-field}} = \frac{\pi J}{2 k_B}, \qquad T_{\text{BKT}}^{\text{MC}} \approx 0.893\, \frac{J}{k_B}$$ $$q_{\square} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\Bigl[\mathrm{wrap}(\theta_{10}-\theta_{00}) + \mathrm{wrap}(\theta_{11}-\theta_{10}) + \mathrm{wrap}(\theta_{01}-\theta_{11}) + \mathrm{wrap}(\theta_{00}-\theta_{01})\Bigr] \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$$

Below $T_{\text{BKT}}$, $F_{\text{vortex}} \to +\infty$ as $L \to \infty$, locking vortices into neutral $+1/-1$ dipole pairs with algebraic correlation decay $\langle \vec{s}_0 \cdot \vec{s}_r \rangle \sim r^{-\eta(T)}$. Above $T_{\text{BKT}}$, $F_{\text{vortex}} \to -\infty$ and free vortices proliferate exponentially.

Why Naive Parallel GPU Metropolis Breaks Detailed Balance: In a standard fragment shader, every texel $(x, y)$ reads the previous frame's neighbors simultaneously. If two adjacent spins both flip in the same pass, the true energy change $\Delta E$ differs from the locally computed $\Delta E$, violating detailed balance and creating artificial checkerboard oscillations! Splitting each Monte Carlo sweep into Pass A (even parity (x + y) % 2 == 0) and Pass B (odd parity (x + y) % 2 == 1) guarantees that no two simultaneously updated spins ever share a bond.
