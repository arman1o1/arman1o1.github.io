Superfluid Vortices in Your Browser: Solving the Gross–Pitaevskii BEC Equation in WebGL

November 14, 2026 • ☕ 12 min read

In Solving Navier–Stokes at 60 FPS, we simulated classical viscous fluids where vorticity $\omega = \nabla \times \vec u$ takes arbitrary continuous values and decays via viscosity. In Quantum Tunneling in Your Browser, we evolved a linear single-particle Schrödinger wavefunction $\psi(\vec x, t) \in \mathbb{C}$. What happens when millions of bosons condense into the exact same macroscopic quantum ground state and repel one another via s-wave contact interactions?

The result is a Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC) superfluid governed by the Gross–Pitaevskii Equation (GPE) (Tsubota et al., arXiv:cond-mat/0104255; Villois et al., arXiv:1604.03595). Adding a single cubic self-interaction term $g|\psi|^2\psi$ to our staggered symplectic leapfrog shader transforms the linear dispersion relation into a Bogoliubov superfluid with zero viscosity, a finite speed of sound $c_s = \sqrt{gn_0/m}$, and topologically quantized vortices whose circulation is locked to integer multiples of $h/m$.

0. Anatomy of a Quantum Superfluid Solver

Governing PDE
$i\hbar \partial_t \psi = \big(-\tfrac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2 + V_{\text{trap}}(\vec x) + g|\psi|^2 - \mu\big)\psi$, where $g \gt 0$ models repulsive s-wave boson scattering and $\mu = gn_0$ is the chemical potential.
Madelung Fluid Map
Writing $\psi = \sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\varphi}$ maps the GPE onto inviscid compressible Euler hydrodynamics with superfluid velocity $\vec v_s = \frac{\hbar}{m}\nabla\varphi$ and Bohm quantum pressure $Q = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt{\rho}}{\sqrt{\rho}}$.
Quantized Circulation
Because $\psi$ is single-valued, the phase $\varphi$ must wind by $2\pi n$ around any closed loop: $\oint_{\mathcal{C}} \vec v_s \cdot d\vec\ell = \frac{h}{m}n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$). Vorticity vanishes everywhere except at point singularities where $\rho \to 0$.
Healing Length $\xi$
The vortex core radius $\xi = \hbar / \sqrt{2mgn_0}$ where kinetic quantum pressure balances nonlinear interaction energy $gn_0$.

1. Madelung Hydrodynamics & Why Superfluid Vortices Are Quantized

Substituting the polar decomposition $\psi(\vec x, t) = \sqrt{\rho(\vec x, t)}\,e^{i\varphi(\vec x, t)}$ (with natural units $\hbar = m = 1$) into the Gross–Pitaevskii equation and separating real and imaginary parts yields the exact Madelung quantum fluid equations:

Madelung Continuity & Quantum Euler Momentum Equations ($\hbar = m = 1$)
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec v_s) = 0, \qquad \frac{\partial \vec v_s}{\partial t} + (\vec v_s \cdot \nabla)\vec v_s = -\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla P_{\text{int}} - \nabla V_{\text{trap}} + \nabla\!\left(\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt{\rho}}{2\sqrt{\rho}}\right)$$ $$\vec v_s = \nabla \varphi = \frac{\psi_R \nabla \psi_I - \psi_I \nabla \psi_R}{\psi_R^2 + \psi_I^2}, \qquad P_{\text{int}} = \frac{1}{2}g\rho^2, \qquad c_s = \sqrt{\frac{\partial P_{\text{int}}}{\partial \rho}} = \sqrt{g\rho_0}$$

Notice two remarkable consequences: (1) the repulsive nonlinearity $g|\psi|^2$ acts as a polytropic gas pressure $P = \frac{1}{2}g\rho^2$ with sound speed $c_s = \sqrt{g\rho_0}$, and (2) the Bohm quantum potential $\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt{\rho}}{2\sqrt{\rho}}$ regularizes vortex cores at scale $\xi = 1/\sqrt{2g\rho_0}$ without requiring artificial viscosity!

Why You Must Subtract the Chemical Potential $\mu = gn_0$ in Explicit Leapfrog Schemes: Without subtracting $\mu = gn_0$ from the diagonal potential, the uniform ground state $\psi_0 = \sqrt{n_0}e^{-i\mu t}$ rotates in complex phase at angular frequency $\omega_0 = gn_0$, eating up your symplectic stability budget $\Delta t \le 2 / (E_{\max} + gn_0)$ and causing high-$g$ condensates to blow up! Working in the co-rotating chemical potential frame ($\hat{H}_{\text{GP}} - \mu$) keeps the uniform background stationary in phase.
