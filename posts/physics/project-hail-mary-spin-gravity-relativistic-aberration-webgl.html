Simulating the Hail Mary: Centrifugal Gravity & Relativistic Aberration at 0.92c

November 26, 2026 • ☕ 11 min read

After reviewing Project Hail Mary (2026), two physics set-pieces stayed stuck in my head: the 1.5g brachistochrone burn powered by Astrophage that accelerates the ship past $0.92c$ on its way to Tau Ceti, and the tethered centrifuge mode where the crew module separates from the fuel tanks and spins on cables to restore Earth gravity in orbit. Both look deceptively simple on screen, yet hiding underneath are two of the cleanest coordinate transformations in classical and relativistic mechanics.

1. Relativistic Aberration & the Forward Starbow

At $\beta = v/c = 0.92$, the Lorentz factor is $\gamma = (1 - \beta^2)^{-1/2} \approx 2.55$. Photons emitted by a star at rest-frame polar angle $\theta$ relative to the ship's velocity vector arrive at an apparent angle $\theta'$ compressed toward the nose of the ship:

Relativistic Aberration, Doppler Factor & Headlight Intensity
$$\cos\theta' = \frac{\cos\theta + \beta}{1 + \beta\cos\theta}, \qquad D(\theta') = \frac{1}{\gamma(1 - \beta\cos\theta')}, \qquad I'_{\text{bol}} = D^4 I_{\text{bol}}$$

Every star ahead of the ship is both pulled forward into a tight cone and blueshifted by $D(0) = \sqrt{(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)} \approx 4.9\times$ at $\beta = 0.92$. A yellow G-type star ($T \approx 5800\,\text{K}$) directly ahead shifts to an effective blackbody temperature of $T' = D \cdot T \approx 28{,}400\,\text{K}$ (searing ultraviolet-blue), while stars behind the ship redshift toward deep infrared and dim by $D^4 \approx 0.0017\times$.

2. Rotating Tether Centrifuge & Coriolis Deflection

Once in orbit around Tau Ceti, the Hail Mary splits along a tether of radius $R \approx 50\,\text{m}$ and spins at angular velocity $\omega = \sqrt{g / R} \approx 0.443\,\text{rad/s}$ ($\approx 4.2\,\text{RPM}$). Inside the rotating cabin frame, a dropped wrench obeys the non-inertial equation of motion:

Equation of Motion in the Rotating Cabin Frame
$$\ddot{\mathbf{r}} = -\underbrace{\boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r})}_{\text{centrifugal } (+\omega^2 \mathbf{r})} - \underbrace{2\boldsymbol{\omega} \times \dot{\mathbf{r}}}_{\text{Coriolis deflection}}$$

Drop a pen from height $h = 2\,\text{m}$ above the deck and it does not hit the floor directly below your hand. Because the pen starts at a smaller radius $R - h$ with lower tangential speed $\omega(R - h)$, it lags behind the faster-moving deck below it – deflecting anti-spinward by $\Delta s \approx \frac{2}{3}\omega \sqrt{2 h^3 / g} \approx 37.7\,\text{cm}$!
